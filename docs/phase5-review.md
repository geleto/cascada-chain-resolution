### Behavior and ownership decision

The oracle is an issued sequential history: earlier reads capture the old logical value, later writes affect only their selected occurrence, and required delivery may suspend either without changing order. A failed key keeps its causal Error and repair baseline. Import publishes complete prepared state once per synchronous segment; prepared references retain their original producer and policy. Closed consumers release their work while shared delivery continues; fatal execution stops both. Unused suffixes are never consumed. External discovery remains finite and static, and snapshots reject nested thenables.

**Outcome: retain the importer/placement split.** [Input preparation](../src/input-preparations.js) owns raw delivery, inspection reuse, boundary policy and atomic staged admission. [Placement versions](../src/property-versions.js) own a different transition: copying or publishing the complete value/presence/order/recovery/discovery facts into independent destinations. A prepared reference bypasses fresh inspection without bypassing its publication. The version can outlive its original container, while a discarded construction has no publication authority. Operation closure cannot own this shared producer. These distinctions remain necessary with Promise keys; the keys delay selection, not the original data producer's authority.

Ranked simplification dispositions:

1. **One importer/publication owner — rejected after tracing the distinct obligations.** It would have to delegate copies, gates and detached captures back to placement code or acquire transaction, destination and consumer lifetimes itself. No removed mechanism offsets those added responsibilities. This answers the architecture question without a production rewrite; the [rejected import-session and universal-completion proposals](phase5-stabilization.md#settled-decisions-and-rejected-shortcuts) have no new evidence that reverses their cost. Preserve segment atomicity, original producers, retirement/re-entry and independent consumer closure. Validation targets complete placement transfer and live shared delivery after closure.
2. **One policy for all receiving routes — rejected as a contract change without a demonstrated benefit.** Raw data rejection and failed operation keys have different causal boundaries; already contextualized poison retains its source. Mutable snapshots must reject nested availability and avoid admitting host storage in place. A generic classifier would hide these distinctions or require the same policies as flags. The six-route differential and directed shared-thenable cases check the existing common preparation boundary plus its necessary policies.
3. **Drop all captures at selection/result completion — rejected by last-use evidence.** Source containers can collect once their placements are captured, while captured versions, recovery baselines, native authority and complete Error collection have different last uses. The new source-release probe confirms release before pending data completes; live controls establish required protection. No additional lifecycle framework or stronger permanent retention is justified. This also gives no new basis for re-proposing Phase 6's reverted single-recovery-owner experiment.

No supported production defect or beneficial replacement has been established. No semantic change or architecture approval is requested. These are scoped design conclusions, not proof that no better representation exists.
