## Actionable findings

### R1 — validation Errors retain unused Array payloads (P2, B17+B18)

An invalid growth operation can finish with `InvalidArrayLength`, and an out-of-range `with` can finish with `InvalidArrayOperation`, while the retained unformatted poison keeps an unused object argument alive after the receiver is cleared. This does not require a large payload or a huge Array: `with(3, payload)` on an Array of length two reproduces the small case. Maximum-length sparse Arrays exercise the growth paths without allocating billions of elements.

The [validation probe](2026-10-25-04-lifetime-overflow.mjs) has 36 cases. Fourteen retain their selected payload: `concat` in all four delivery modes; `push`, `unshift`, `splice`, `toSpliced` and `with` for ready and synchronous delivery. The receiver and its child collect. Healthy discarded results collect, and explicitly retained healthy results keep their payload. Deferred payloads that never need consumption by an already rejected operation are separate controls.

The actual retaining paths are the poison's lazy stack frames through spent runtime closures:

- `prepareArrayReceiver` in `src/array-invocation.js`: `finish` retains `args` after invalid growth.
- `prepareConcatArguments` in `src/array-methods.js`: the callback retains `parts` after combined length validation fails.
- `prepareWithArguments` in the same file: `finish` retains `replacement` after the bounds failure.

The correction should end each capture at its actual validation/handoff boundary while preserving pending successful consumers. Clearing InvocationWork's input fields alone does not clear these local bindings. Formatting stacks or changing the returned diagnostic would conceal the lifetime defect rather than fix it.
